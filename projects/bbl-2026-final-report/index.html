BBL 2026 Final — Post-Match Coaching Report

Three tactical findings from a hand-coded final, written for a coach rather than an analyst.

Smith against pace in the final. Each line is one shot, coloured by runs scored.

The question

Sydney Thunder made 189 batting first in the BBL 2026 Final and lost. Where did the match actually turn?

What I found

1. Thunder found the way to contain Smith, and used it too late.

Steve Smith scored at a strike rate of 341 against right-arm pace, with seven sixes. Tanveer Sangha's leg spin held him to a strike rate of 100, with five dots in twelve balls.

Smith vs right-arm pace 341 strike rate, 7 sixes
Smith vs Sangha 100 strike rate, 5 dots in 12 balls

2. Hadley kept bowling to Smith's strength without adjusting.

Ryan Hadley bowled good length outside off to Smith eight times and conceded 26 runs from that one zone. Four of those eight balls went for six.

3. Green showed how to stop Babar. Nobody else followed.

Babar Azam scored 47 off 39, largely because Thunder kept offering width outside off stump, his main scoring zone. Chris Green held him to a strike rate of 80, with nine dots in fifteen balls, by bowling a tight middle-stump line. No other bowler did the same.

How it was built

  • Hand-coded data. All 239 deliveries of the final, with 28 variables per ball: line, length, shot played, intended and actual direction, ball speed, batter handedness, and dismissal detail.
  • Charts built from scratch. Beehive and wagon wheel charts in Python, including mirroring for left-handers and the boundary geometry.
  • Written for coaching staff. Each finding leads with the insight, uses a visual as proof, and ends with a recommended action.
